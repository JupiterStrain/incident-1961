STATION PROTOCOL MANUAL - EXTRACT
RECOVERY STATION KESTREL

Extract of sections bearing on biological work. Full manual held at the
Director's office and at █████████████████.

SECTION 1 - THE STATION

Recovery Station Kestrel is a receiving and quarantine facility of the
Office of Civil Aerospace Contingency. It is not a launch facility, is
not a laboratory of record, and does not hold or issue findings.

The station's biological work is conducted under Project APEX (Aerospace
Primate Examination), authorised █████████████, scope as stated in the
authorising instrument: the examination of primates returned from
sub-orbital and orbital flight.

Staff establishment: ███. Levels ██ to ██.
Quarantine bays 1 through 4, Level ██.

SECTION 4 - DESIGNATIONS

Specimens are designated by number in order of receipt. Personnel are
designated by employee number in all written record.

Names are not used in the file. This is long-standing practice and its
purpose is administrative continuity, not secrecy. A number does not
resign, transfer, marry or die.

SECTION 6 - THE DAILY MEASUREMENT

One measurement, one technician, one hour, one instrument. The instrument
does not leave the bay.

Where a measurement is not taken, the entry is MEASUREMENT DECLINED and
the reason is stated. A blank is not permitted. A blank in a series is
read by the next reader as an oversight, and this station has had one
occasion on which that would have been the wrong reading.

SECTION 9 - CORRESPONDENCE AND SPEECH

Station business is not discussed outside the perimeter.

Station business is not discussed inside the perimeter within hearing of
an occupied bay. See Handling Instruction 7-A, step seven.

SECTION 11 - DEPARTURE

Personnel leaving at the end of a shift pass ███████████████████ and are
logged out by number.

The log records that a person left. It does not record what left with
them, and the Director has asked that this sentence be retained in the
manual in exactly this form.

SECTION 14 - VISITORS

Visitors are not received during an active quarantine.

The veterinary examination of 19 November 1961 was conducted by a
practitioner brought in from outside the establishment and given no
briefing. The purpose was an unprejudiced opinion and the opinion
obtained was that the animal was healthy.

The practitioner was correct. The procedure was sound. Both facts are
recorded here because the file will otherwise be read as though somebody
made a mistake that day, and nobody did.
