MEDICAL FOLLOW-UP - EMPLOYEE 4119
EXAMINATIONS OF 24 AND 30 NOVEMBER AND 6 DECEMBER 1961

PRESENTING HISTORY

Employee 4119 was in the corridor outside Bay 3 at approximately 1400 on
23 November. He reported no injury on the day and has reported none
since.

Five of the six objects expelled were recovered from the walls of Bay 3.
The sixth has not been recovered.

EXAMINATION, 24 NOVEMBER

No wound. No puncture. No entry mark. Skin intact on every surface
examined, and the examination was thorough.

Chest sounds clear. Abdomen soft. Reflexes normal. No lymphadenopathy.

Temperature elevated one degree. Attributed at the time to the length of
the shift and the conditions in the plant room.

EXAMINATION, 30 NOVEMBER

No wound.

Temperature elevated two degrees. Patient reports feeling well and has
worked every day since. Supervisor confirms attendance and confirms the
standard of work is unchanged.

Weight down ██████ pounds. Patient attributes this to the
lunchroom being shut.

EXAMINATION, 6 DECEMBER

No wound.

Temperature elevated four degrees and holding.

Patient is in good health by every measure available to me. Color good.
Appetite good. Sleeping normally. He asked after my daughter by name,
which I had not mentioned to him, and which I mention here only because
the file is required to be complete.

Cranial circumference recorded at his own suggestion. He offered. He said
he had heard we were doing that now.

    6 DECEMBER      ████ CM

I have entered the figure and I have not entered a baseline, because
there is no baseline for this man. Nobody measured him before.

ASSESSMENT

I cannot make a finding of injury. There is no wound and there has never
been a wound, and I have looked three times.

I am not able to certify this man fit and I am not able to certify him
unfit, and I have said so to the Director in those words.

The elevation is four degrees. It has stopped moving.

That figure appears twice in this station's records. This is the second
time.

RECOMMENDATION

Weekly examination. Cranial circumference at each. Same instrument, same
hour, same officer.

He has agreed to all of it without hesitation and is entirely willing to
come in. He is the easiest patient on this station.
