RECOVERY DETACHMENT - DECK LOG - EXTRACT

Extract covers 0600 to 1000, 16 November 1961. Full log held at
████████████████████. This extract prepared for the Kestrel file.

0604    Detachment at station. Sea state 2. Visibility good.
0730    Notified of retrofire. Predicted area ██████████████████.
0808    Contact. Two parachutes observed, main fully deployed.
0812    Splashdown. Bearing ██████, range 2.1 miles.
0819    Vessel alongside at 400 yards. Beacon strong and continuous.
0827    Swimmers away. Collar fitted 0834. Vehicle stable, upright.
0851    Vehicle aboard by boom. No water taken. Hull dry.
0903    Hatch opened by ███████████ in presence of medical officer.

REMARKS ON OPENING

Occupant alive and alert. Sitting up in the couch. Not agitated. Medical
officer's word at the time was "bored", which is in his own log and which
I am repeating here because it is a better description than any of the
official ones.

Occupant accepted fruit at 0907. Handled without restraint. Did not
require the pole and did not require the second man.

I have recovered eleven animals from the water. This is the first one
that did not have to be persuaded out of the couch.

0917    Occupant to transport container.
0928    Container to helicopter.
0944    Detachment released.

CONDITION OF VEHICLE

Hull intact. No breach, no scoring, no deformation of the pressure
bulkhead. Heat shield within tolerance and retained. Two seals were
checked at the hatch and both were good.

Detachment recorded no anomaly of any kind.

APPENDED NOTE, 29 NOVEMBER 1961

This detachment has now been interviewed three times regarding the
condition of the hull. The answer has not changed and will not change.

The vehicle was sound. If the intent of the questions is to establish
that something entered the cabin through a fault in this vehicle, this
detachment is unable to assist, because there was no fault.

We are told the relevant interval is the flight itself and not the
recovery. If that is so, then whatever is being looked for was aboard
long before this detachment saw the parachutes, and no inspection of a
hull was ever going to find it.
