VEHICLE TEARDOWN - HULL AND SEAL INSPECTION

Vehicle ███████████████████, flown 16 November 1961. Teardown conducted
19-21 November at █████████████████.

SCOPE

To establish whether the cabin was breached at any point between launch
and hatch opening.

FINDINGS

    Pressure hull            Intact. No perforation. No crack.
    Heat shield              Retained. Ablation within tolerance.
    Hatch seal, primary      Good. Compression set within limits.
    Hatch seal, secondary    Good.
    Umbilical penetration    Good.
    Instrument penetrations  ██████ examined. All good.
    Cabin pressure record    Continuous. No excursion.

The cabin did not depressurise, did not repressurise, and did not vent at
any point in the profile. The record is continuous for the full 4 hours
11 minutes and shows nothing.

CONCLUSION AS REQUESTED

There was no breach.

REMARK BY THE INSPECTOR

I have been asked to establish how a contaminant entered a sealed cabin.
I am reporting that I cannot, and I would like to record why, because the
question as put to me cannot be answered by a teardown.

This hull was built to hold pressure. Every seal on it was specified,
tested and certified against pressure differential. A seal that holds
pressure is not the same article as a seal that excludes ████████████████████████,
and nobody has ever asked this division to build the second kind because
until this month nobody had any reason to.

If the contaminant is of the order of magnitude I have been told it is -
and I have been told very little - then this vehicle was never sealed
against it, no vehicle we have ever built was sealed against it, and the
teardown I have just completed was the wrong test performed very
carefully.

I recommend the question be restated. Not "where is the hole." There is
no hole. The question is what the cabin was █████████████ through for
four hours, and that is a question for ███████████████████ and not for
Engineering.
